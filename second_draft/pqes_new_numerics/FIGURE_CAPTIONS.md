# Draft numerical captions and precise required computations

These captions are part of the simulation handoff, not automatically inserted into the Overleaf manuscript. The figure references are ordered A–E for the new technical section; panel designs may be adjusted before submission. Each PDF is generated by `plot_results.py` from the CSV files generated by `run_simulations.py`. Model results are exact within the stated finite-dimensional Markovian circuit model, while the phase-diagram sample counts are large-shot approximations. **Do not claim model-independent hardware superiority.**

## Figure A — Controlled SWAP record noise, architecture comparisons and estimator validation

File: `figures/pqes_new_numerics/PQES_FigA_readout_and_instrument_noise.pdf`.

**Draft caption.** Physical apparatus noise in branch-resolved recursive SWAP virtual distillation and a coherent-control streaming baseline. Data are generated by exact density-matrix propagation through controlled register SWAPs with a common native Fredkin circuit model, and the final estimator uses the reported SWAP parity and a projective measurement of the known target projector. **(a)** The relative signed normalization `t(q)/t(0)` as a function of independent classical assignment error `q` in every control measurement, comparing a measured accumulator (N−1 separately measured controls) with one coherent ancilla measured only at the end. **(b)** The resulting variance ratio `v_measured/v_coherent` for identical physical preparations, compared with the exact independent-readout prediction `(1−2q)^[−2(N−2)]`. **(c)** Apparatus-error sensitivity of the four architectures under separately applied native-Fredkin depolarization, ancilla Z noise, control idle dephasing, data memory dephasing, measurement assignment errors, reset errors, and their combination, normalized to the ideal-apparatus variance. **(d)** Direct sampling of control-parity and final observable outcomes as a function of the number of shots, compared with the predicted central-limit standard error `sqrt(v/S)`. Statistical sampling is used only in (d); other panels give exact instrument moments in the specified CPTP model. All parameters, including source-state noise and native-gate error channels, are saved in the CSV files. Results are not attributed to a particular hardware platform.

**Do not claim:** that a locally measured ancilla always improves precision; independent readout errors create a clear counterexample.

## Figure B — Width-constrained resource-phase diagram

File: `figures/pqes_new_numerics/PQES_FigB_architecture_phase_diagram.pdf`.

**Draft caption.** Width-constrained comparison of parallel balanced, depth-first recycled, and sequential measured-SWAP trees with a coherent-control low-width VD implementation, for `M=2`, power `N=4` and an independently prepared nonmaximally entangled target subjected to stochastic local Pauli depolarization. The horizontal axis is the probability `q` of misreporting each ancilla measurement, and the vertical axis is extra stochastic control-phase-flip probability accumulated between successive register exchanges by the coherent-control implementation. Native three-body Fredkin depolarization, per-Fredkin control noise, data memory dephasing and reset errors are held fixed at the values stated in `phase.csv`; the resource constraint is a peak coherent width of at most seven qubits. **(a)** The architecture with the smallest estimated total number of Fredkin gates needed for an RMSE target of 0.12, with ties resolved by smaller peak width and then depth; infeasible high-bias or over-width implementations are excluded. **(b)** The ratio of estimated total Fredkin costs for the measured sequential accumulator and coherent-control VD. **(c)** Per-shot delta-method variance coefficient `v_A` versus coherent idle error at fixed `q`. **(d)** Corresponding total estimated Fredkin counts. The shot requirement uses the leading large-sample variance and checks the systematic-bias floor; these are asymptotic planning metrics, not rigorous finite-shot CIs. Different hardware noise or latency models can change the optimal architecture.

**Additional validation before a publication-level hardware claim:** calibrated native-gate compilation, explicit latency and physical reset, and finite-shot confidence intervals.

## Figure C — Mid-circuit versus output-only state virtual distillation

File: `figures/pqes_new_numerics/PQES_FigC_midcircuit_placement.pdf`.

**Draft caption.** Comparison of weighted-state compression after the first block of a fixed known entangling algorithm with virtual distillation applied to the final noisy output. The ideal computation is decomposed as `U=U_B U_A`; the downstream block `U_B` is repeated a specified number of times, with local CPTP noise after every algorithmic gate. Both the measured SWAP accumulator and coherent-control streaming VD are allowed the **same** intermediate compression location. The physical mid-circuit weighted operator after the second block is `Lambda_B(rho_A^N)` while output-only VD represents `[Lambda_B(rho_A)]^N`. **(a)** Absolute estimator bias relative to the noiseless target observable (the known ideal output projector). **(b)** Total algorithmic gate executions per experimental shot, excluding distillation instruments, for intermediate versus output-only placement. **(c)** Delta-method sampling-variance coefficient. **(d)** Magnitude of the actual signed normalization, including noise in the distillation instrument. The reduction in downstream algorithmic gates is a general property of nondestructive weighted-state implementations, not a uniquely PQES capability. Actual estimation cost must combine bias, variance and all physical gates.

**Do not claim:** that mid-circuit compression always improves fidelity. Downstream noise remains after intermediate compression.

## Figure D — Independent random-frame twirling versus correlated-frame protocols

File: `figures/pqes_new_numerics/PQES_FigD_randomized_twirling.pdf`.

**Draft caption.** Monte Carlo frame effects for a `|+>` qubit under stochastic Z-dephasing. Conjugation frames rotate the physical Z error among X, Y and Z axes. **(a)** The virtual fidelity obtained with independently chosen frames for each consumed input copy, corresponding to `(E_f rho_f)^N`, and the different fidelity obtained when all copies share one randomly chosen frame, corresponding to `E_f(rho_f^N)`. **(b)** Mean absolute fidelity approximation error of a *fixed empirical K-frame averaged channel*, with between-frame-set uncertainty, measured relative to the exact independently twirled power state. **(c)** Distribution of the empirical K-frame virtual fidelities at selected small/large K, together with the exact-twirl value. **(d)** Signed normalization under independent and shared randomization. Unlike a fixed K-frame approximation, an implementation that resamples an independent uniform frame for each incoming copy need not enumerate the complete `3^M` ensemble. The chosen K values and independent frame-set seeds are recorded in the CSV data.

**Do not claim:** that a fixed percentage of the full Clifford ensemble is a canonical Monte Carlo error model.

## Figure E — Ideal power-state fidelity versus repeated-cycle costs

File: `figures/pqes_new_numerics/PQES_FigE_repeated_cycle_operational_overhead.pdf`.

**Draft caption.** Formal normalized iteration of local depolarizing noise followed by an ideal power-N map, for a known pure target, showing **(a)** normalized virtual-state fidelity, **(b)** logarithm of the signed normalization `Z_t`, **(c)** the `N^t` independent input copies required for one direct nested signed-ensemble trajectory, and **(d)** the conservative inverse-square normalization sampling factor `Z_t^{-2}`. The weighted physical implementation must retain all earlier parity signs; the state shown in (a) is not the deterministic physical output of a single recovery register. The rapidly growing resource factors distinguish formal spectral stability from operational quantum-memory error correction.

**Do not claim:** a quantum error-correction memory threshold from (a) alone.

## Appendix figure — Ideal Pauli-channel power filter (VCP-related proxy)

File: `figures/pqes_new_numerics/PQES_App_IdealVCP_channel_proxy_ONLY.pdf`.

**Draft caption.** **Ideal-channel-only** reference for layerwise Pauli channel purification. At each single-qubit stochastic Pauli error location, channel probabilities `(1-p,p/3,p/3,p/3)` are raised to a chosen power and renormalized before their application to a known target circuit. This illustrates spectral performance of the ideal channel-purification object in a restricted Pauli setting, but does not simulate the physical VCP protocol, its control registers, sampling cost, Choi-state resources or noisy gate compilation. It is therefore **not** a valid matched resource-efficiency comparison with physical SWAP circuits.


## Figure F — Physical order-two VCP versus nondestructive state VD

File: `figures/pqes_new_numerics/PQES_FigF_physical_layerwise_VCP2_vs_stateVD2.pdf`.

**Draft caption.** Comparison of a physically implemented order-two layerwise virtual-channel-purification (VCP2) instrument with two order-two virtual-state-distillation (VD2) instruments on the same known single-/two-qubit algorithm subjected to stochastic local Pauli noise. For each algorithmic gate-noise location, the VCP2 instrument prepares a maximally mixed auxiliary register, performs one controlled register swap, applies two independent copies of the original Pauli channel on the data and auxiliary registers, performs a second controlled register swap, and measures its control qubit in the X basis. Successive signed physical branches are propagated across circuit layers; no normalized power state is substituted as a deterministic physical register. The state-VD2 baselines instead prepare two independent completed noisy algorithm outputs, perform a single nondestructive register SWAP test and accumulate their signed output. Noise after native three-qubit Fredkins, ancilla dephasing and readout assignment errors are applied according to the same instrument model. **(a)** The systematic bias of the signed estimator relative to the ideal final target projector. **(b)** The leading per-shot variance coefficient. **(c)** The signed normalization. **(d)** The number of controlled Fredkin gates per trajectory. The distinct resource types must also be reported: layerwise VCP2 uses two noisy-channel calls per circuit-noise location, one principal target-state preparation and two controlled register swaps per location, while state VD2 uses two complete noisy algorithm preparations and one controlled register swap at the output. The comparison is restricted to order two and stochastic Pauli channels; the auxiliary I/d state, algorithmic gates, final target-projector measurement and timing costs remain idealized.

**Required before generalizing:** compare implementations under matched native two-qubit gate compilation, auxiliary preparation cost and realistic correlated/coherent noise. Higher-order VCP is not implied by these results.
